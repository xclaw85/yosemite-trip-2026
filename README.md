| # | Check | Why it matters |

## ⚠️ Heat advisory

The National Weather Service forecasts **high risk of extreme heat in the Central Valley Aug 1–2** and **moderate risk of extreme heat extending into the Sierra Nevada Aug 1–4.** Central Valley highs of 105–113°F. This is the hottest stretch California has seen in roughly two years, and it lands squarely on your arrival and first two full days.

### Estimated Yosemite Valley conditions (4,000 ft)

| Day | Est. high | Est. low | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sun Aug 2 · arrival | 98–103°F | ~62°F | Peak heat. Your drive crosses the Central Valley at 105–113°F. |
| Mon Aug 3 | 97–102°F | ~62°F | Still peak |
| Tue Aug 4 | 95–100°F | ~60°F | Last day of the ridge |
| Wed Aug 5 | 91–96°F | ~58°F | Cooling begins |
| Thu Aug 6 · depart | 89–94°F | ~57°F | Near normal for August |

> These are estimates built from the NWS heat outlook plus August climatology (normal Valley high is around 89°F). This is 6+ days out and the position of the high-pressure system is still uncertain. **Re-check weather.gov the night before you leave.**

**What it changes:**

- The unheated canvas tent will be miserable from roughly 1 PM to 6 PM. Plan to be in the river or at the pool.
- Nights in the low 60s are mild, but still bring a sleeping bag. Provided bedding is thin.
- Check coolant, radiator, and tire pressure before departure. The CA-58 Tehachapi grade and the Hwy 41 climb are where cars overheat at 110°F.
- Carry a gallon of water for the car on top of your drinking water.
- Consider leaving Lake Arrowhead at 5:30 AM instead of 6:30 to clear Bakersfield and Fresno before the worst.

## 🚧 Mist Trail closure

From **July 27 through October 2026**, the Mist Trail from its junction with the John Muir Trail (just above the Vernal Fall footbridge) to the top of Vernal Fall is:

- ✅ **Open** Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and holidays
- ❌ **Closed Monday through Thursday, 7:00 AM to 3:30 PM**
- ✅ Normally open Mon–Thu from 3:30 PM to 7:00 AM

Your full days are Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday. All affected.

### Getting to Vernal Fall anyway

| Option | Route | Distance / gain | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John Muir Trail** | Up the JMT to Clark Point, then the cutoff down to the top of Vernal Fall | 5.4 mi RT / ~1,300 ft | ✅ **Best given the heat.** Legal any hour. Start at 5:45 AM, done by 11. |
| **Afternoon Mist Trail** | Start at Happy Isles at 3:30 PM when the section reopens | 2.4 mi RT / 1,000 ft | ⚠️ Only if the heat has broken. Wed Aug 5 is the better bet. Bring headlamps. |
| **Footbridge only** | Turn around at the Vernal Fall footbridge | 1.6 mi RT / 400 ft | ✅ Good bail-out. Never closed, classic head-on view of the fall. |

Expectation setting: **there is very little actual mist in August.** The falls run on late-season snowmelt. Still spectacular, just not the drenching-spray version in the photos.

Confirm current status at the Valley Welcome Center on arrival. Details: [nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadatrail.htm](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/vernalnevadatrail.htm)

## 🥾 Hike reference

### Easy afternoon trio, Glacier Point Road (best in heat)

7,000 to 7,700 ft, runs 10 to 15 degrees cooler than the Valley.

| Hike | Distance | Gain | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glacier Point overlook | 0.6 mi RT, paved | minimal | 30 min |
| Sentinel Dome | 2.2 mi RT | ~400 ft | 1.5 hrs |
| Taft Point & the Fissures | 2.2 mi RT | ~250 ft | 1.5 hrs |

### Easy afternoon trio, Valley floor

| Hike | Distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bridalveil Fall | 0.5 mi RT, paved | Still has flow in August |
| Mirror Lake | 2 mi RT, paved | Shaded along Tenaya Creek, best Half Dome view |
| Cook's Meadow Loop | 1 mi, flat boardwalk | Do it at golden hour |

Chain them with a swim at Swinging Bridge or Sentinel Beach.

> **Skip Lower Yosemite Fall.** It is the most popular easy walk in the park, but **Yosemite Falls is typically a trickle or bone dry by early August.**

### Mariposa Grove (on the drive home)

Mariposa Grove Road is closed to private vehicles. Park at the **Welcome Plaza** and ride the free shuttle (roughly 8 AM to 7 PM, confirm on arrival).

| Trail | Distance | Gain | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Trees Loop | 0.3 mi | flat, accessible | 20 min |
| **Grizzly Giant Loop** | 2 mi | ~300 ft | 1.5 hrs |
| Guardians Loop | 6.5 mi | ~1,200 ft | 4–5 hrs (too much for a travel day) |

Grizzly Giant Loop is the pick: the Grizzly Giant, the Bachelor and Three Graces, and the California Tunnel Tree. Shaded, at 5,600 ft.

## 🎒 Packing checklist

Specific to an unheated canvas tent cabin.

- [ ] **Sleeping bag or extra blanket** (provided bedding is thin)
- [ ] **Headlamp or flashlight per person** (Curry Village is genuinely dark)
- [ ] **Power banks** (unheated tents have one light bulb and **no outlets**)
- [ ] **Padlock** for the bear locker
- [ ] **Shower shoes** (shared bathhouse, and it is a walk)
- [ ] **Earplugs** (canvas is not soundproof, tents are close together)
- [ ] Reusable water bottles, 2L capacity each
- [ ] Hiking shoes, not sandals
- [ ] Sunscreen, hat, sunglasses
- [ ] Rain layer and one warm layer for evenings
- [ ] Folding wagon if you can get one (everything is hauled from the car)
- [ ] Closeable plastic containers for the food locker
- [ ] Gallon of water for the car
- [ ] Offline maps downloaded

### Bear rules, not optional

No food, drinks, toiletries, or trash in the tent or the car. Everything goes in the bear locker. **Fines run up to $5,000** and vehicles used for food storage can be impounded. Cooking is prohibited anywhere in Curry Village.
