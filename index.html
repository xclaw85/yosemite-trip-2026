⚠ Heat advisory for your exact dates

The National Weather Service forecasts high risk of extreme heat in the Central Valley August 1–2 and moderate extreme-heat risk in the Sierra Nevada August 1–4. Central Valley highs of 105–113°F. This is the hottest stretch California has seen in about two years, and it lands on your drive-in day and your first full day.

Plan around it: hike at dawn, gain elevation midday, be in the river by 1 PM. Re-check weather.gov the night before you leave.

Gear

Packing list

Specific to an unheated canvas tent cabin. Tick as you go.

Bear rules are not optional. No food, drinks, toiletries, or trash in the tent or the car. Everything goes in the bear locker. Fines run up to $5,000 and vehicles used for food storage can be impounded. Cooking is prohibited anywhere in Curry Village.

Do this first

Three things to verify

Any one of these can derail the trip. Sort them before you pack.

01

Is Curry Village actually booked?

August is peak season. Call (888) 413-8869 and confirm you have a confirmation number, not a browsing session. Backups: Housekeeping Camp, Yosemite Valley Lodge, or outside the park in El Portal, Midpines, Mariposa, or Oakhurst.

02

Confirm the bed configuration

Tent cabins come as 1 double, or 1 double + 1 single, or 1 double + 3 singles. Roll-away beds and cribs are not available. Three people in a two-bed unit means someone shares a double. Make sure the third person is on the reservation.

03

The Mist Trail is closed on your weekdays

Trail repairs run July 27 through October. The section above the Vernal Fall footbridge is closed Monday through Thursday, 7:00 AM to 3:30 PM. There is a workaround, see below.

Deposit is 100% of the first night at booking, and cancellations need 7 or more days notice. At this point, treat the booking as non-refundable.

What the heat changes

  • The unheated canvas tent will be miserable from roughly 1 PM to 6 PM. Be in the river or at the pool.
  • Nights in the low 60s are mild, but still bring a sleeping bag. Provided bedding is thin.
  • Check coolant, radiator, and tire pressure before you leave. The CA-58 Tehachapi grade and the Hwy 41 climb are where cars overheat at 110°F.
  • Carry a gallon of water for the car on top of your drinking water.
  • Consider leaving Lake Arrowhead at 5:30 AM instead of 6:30 to clear Bakersfield and Fresno before the worst.

If something goes wrong

Emergency

Cell service in the Valley is patchy to nonexistent. When you have a signal, 911 texts often get through where calls fail. Screenshot this section before you lose service.

Life-threatening emergency Call or TEXT 911 Medical, fire, search & rescue, or crime. Tell dispatch you are in Yosemite Valley and give your nearest landmark or shuttle stop number.
Park dispatch (non-emergency) (209) 379-1992 Rangers, lost persons, wildlife problems, anything urgent that isn't 911.
Yosemite Medical Clinic (209) 372-4637 In Yosemite Valley, 9000 Ahwahnee Dr, between Yosemite Village and The Ahwahnee. Urgent care walk-ins, roughly weekday afternoons only. Not an ER. Out-of-state insurance = pay out of pocket. Call to confirm today's hours.

Nearest hospitals with an ER

The clinic in the Valley is not an emergency room. For anything serious, 911 dispatch will arrange transport and ask your hospital preference. All of these are 2 to 3+ hours away, so mountain response times are long. This is why you turn back early on a hike if someone is struggling.

On the drive: Oakhurst has closer care

Coming or going on Hwy 41, Oakhurst is about an hour from the Valley and has walk-in urgent care plus a small hospital nearby in Mariposa. For a sprain or a bad cut on the way home, this beats driving all the way to Fresno.

Oakhurst Urgent Care (559) 683-2117 48677 Victoria Ln, Oakhurst. Weekday daytime hours. Call first.
Mountain Crisis Services 1-888-966-2350 24-hour, free, confidential. Support and transport for anyone in crisis.

Before you lose signal

  • Text one person back home your daily plan each morning. If you go quiet past a set time, they know roughly where to send help.
  • On any hike, agree on a turnaround time and honor it. Rescue from the Vernal Fall trail is slow and expensive.
  • In this heat, heat exhaustion is the likeliest problem: dizziness, nausea, headache, cramps. Get in shade, sip water, cool the neck and wrists. If someone stops sweating, gets confused, or passes out, that's heat stroke — call 911 immediately.
  • Save these numbers to your phone now, and to the phones of both girls, while you still have service at home.
  • Note your tent cabin number and your parking area at check-in. You'll need them to direct anyone to you.
